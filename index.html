STaaS — Ceph storage-as-a-service architecture

Architecture, sizing and client presentation

Designed the initial large-scale service and then reworked it to fit the client’s available budget while retaining clear protection, failure-domain and expansion decisions.

  • Initial service design. Proposed a 30 PB raw Ceph platform for S3, CephFS and NFS with enterprise SAS SSD/NVMe, EC 4+2 for the capacity tier, three-way replicated NVMe for metadata/index/log and small latency-sensitive data, and a 20% operating reserve.
  • Capacity and physical topology. Calculated replication and EC 4+2/8+4 overhead. After reserve, the initial 30 PB baseline yields about 8 PB sellable with three-way replication or 16 PB with EC 4+2. Compared roughly 82 nodes with 24×15.36 TB SAS SSD against 41 nodes with 24×30.72 TB NVMe, accounting for rack density, power/cooling, switch ports and CRUSH failure domains.
  • Role and access separation. Sized OSD/data, MON, MGR, MDS, RGW and NFS-Ganesha roles separately. Developed distinct public S3, VPN-only NFS, integration and administration paths, with HAProxy/ECMP/BGP-anycast concepts, a 100/200 GbE-class service fabric, isolated recovery/replication traffic and out-of-band administration.
  • Budget-led redesign. Reworked the same service for at least 300 TB sellable using 10 data nodes across three racks, rack-aware EC 4+2 and separate metadata/control/RGW roles. The 614.40 TB raw baseline becomes 409.60 TB after EC and about 327.68 TB sellable after reserve, with a disk-expansion path to roughly 409.60 TB sellable.
  • Client explanation. Produced detailed proposals and diagrams and explained every layer: service roles, protection arithmetic, metadata tiers, rack failure behavior, network fabrics, recovery and staged expansion. Kept local availability and site-level DR as distinct decisions in the discussion.
